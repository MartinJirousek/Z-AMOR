In modern systematics, the historical classification into monocotyledons (monocots) and dicotyledons (dicots) has been fundamentally redefined by molecular phylogenetics. Monocots represent a strictly monophyletic group that evolutionary emerged through a specialized divergence from within the early dicots. Consequently, the traditional dicots are recognized as a paraphyletic grade rather than a natural group. This is evidenced by the existence of early-diverging lineages, known as basal angiosperms (such as Amborellales, Nymphaeales, and Austrobaileyales) and magnoliids, which are evolutionarily older and branched off before the final split between the monocots and the true core dicots (eudicots).
